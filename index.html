I looked at monthly player counts for 1,258 Steam games from 2016 to 2021, asking the kinds of questions a product team would: when are players most active, what happened in 2020, and which games manage to keep their players?

Method and limits

How I did it

Method

I loaded the public TidyTuesday Steam dataset (83,631 game-month rows) into SQLite and wrote SQL to build a clean monthly table and a fixed group of games tracked every month from January 2016 to February 2021. Using the same group keeps totals comparable over time, because the dataset adds new games as the years go on. Every query is in analysis.sql.

SELECT year, ROUND(SUM(avg_players) / 1e6, 2) AS players_millions
FROM monthly
WHERE gamename IN (SELECT gamename FROM cohort)
  AND year IN (2019, 2020) AND mo IN (3, 4)
GROUP BY year;
Limits
  • This is Steam only, and it measures players online, not revenue or purchases.
  • The data covers a sample of 1,258 games, weighted toward games that stayed popular, so staying-power numbers are likely higher than for all games.
  • There is no genre or price field, so I described game types from their names and my own knowledge of the games.
  • The 2020 surge coincided with stay-at-home orders, but this data cannot prove that was the cause.
